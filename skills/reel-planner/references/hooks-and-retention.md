# Hooks and retention

## Hook definition

The hook is the first meaningful reason to continue watching.

It may be visual, informational, emotional, sonic, or a combination.

## Preferred sports hook types

### Peak action
Open on the decisive or extraordinary moment.

### Reaction
Emotion first, context second.

### Outcome
Score/result immediately, then show how it happened.

### High-stakes context
Minimal text that changes how the upcoming footage is understood.

### Visual tension
A moment whose outcome is not immediately clear.

### Stat with proof
A surprising statistic supported immediately by relevant footage.

### Quote
One short line that creates genuine stakes.

### Natural-sound interruption
Hoofbeats, impact, crowd, whistle, commentary, silence before a decisive moment.

## Weak/default hooks

Avoid unless evidence supports:
- logo reveal;
- generic animated title;
- "You won't believe...";
- "POV:";
- "Wait until the end";
- countdown with no meaningful reason;
- question that merely restates the topic;
- fake urgency.

## Retention mechanics

Use:
- immediate relevance;
- visual change with narrative reason;
- unanswered but legitimate question;
- escalating stakes;
- concise context;
- replay/angle reveal;
- reaction;
- consequence.

Do not use:
- random zooms;
- fake progress bars;
- constant text movement;
- meaningless transitions;
- hidden payoff.

## Hook testing

When practical, propose 2–3 hook variants only if they represent materially different hypotheses.

Example:
A. peak action first
B. reaction first
C. result first

Do not create variants that differ only in wording.
