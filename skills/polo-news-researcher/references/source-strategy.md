# Source strategy

## Principle

Use a contextual source graph, not a fixed one-dimensional hierarchy.

A source can be excellent for one type of fact and weak for another.

Examples:
- a governing body is strong for official rules, schedules, sanctions, and tournament records;
- a player account can be primary evidence for a personal announcement;
- a tournament organizer can be primary for venue or fixture changes;
- specialist media may provide original reporting before an official statement;
- a ranking organization is the correct source for its own ranking methodology and current table.

## Source roles

Tag sources with one or more roles:

- `primary_official`
- `primary_participant`
- `first_party_polo_connect`
- `specialist_original_reporting`
- `trusted_secondary_reporting`
- `data_or_ranking`
- `aggregator`
- `social_post`
- `archive`
- `unknown`

Roles describe provenance, not automatic truth.

## Source quality dimensions

Assess:
- proximity to the event;
- authority over the specific fact;
- original vs copied reporting;
- timestamp and recency;
- update/correction behavior;
- transparency of sourcing;
- consistency with independent evidence;
- historical reliability when known.

## Discovery vs confirmation

Discovery sources can be broad.

Confirmation sources should be as close as practical to the underlying fact.

A social post or aggregator may alert the system to a story but should not automatically become the final authority for a consequential claim.

## Independent corroboration

Two sources are independent only if they do not derive the claim from the same original source.

Look for:
- identical unusual wording;
- explicit attribution;
- matching typo/error;
- same quoted anonymous source;
- publication timing suggesting republication.

## First-party Polo Connect evidence

When authorized, prefer internal:
- live scores;
- official production rundowns;
- schedules;
- accreditation information;
- rights metadata;
- match footage;
- internal tournament records;
- direct organizer communications.

Internal evidence still needs timestamps and provenance.
