# Content pillars and recurring series

## Pillars

Pillars are broad editorial territories.

A useful pillar:
- serves a stable audience need;
- supports Polo Connect's identity;
- contains many possible stories;
- can evolve across formats.

Possible territories to evaluate, not mandatory defaults:

- live competition and decisive moments;
- tournament context and results;
- players, teams, and personalities;
- horses and equestrian dimension;
- education and polo literacy;
- data and analysis;
- access / behind-the-scenes;
- culture, history, and lifestyle;
- community;
- Polo Connect viewing/product utility.

Do not create ten equal pillars merely for completeness.

## Pillar definition

For each active pillar record:
- `name`;
- `audience_need`;
- `strategic_role`;
- `polo_connect_advantage`;
- `example_story_types`;
- `anti_pattern`;
- `current_emphasis`.

Emphasis:
- `core`
- `supporting`
- `opportunistic`
- `reduced`

Emphasis is not a percentage.

## Pillar overlap

One post may serve multiple pillars.

Do not force exclusive classification.

## Series

A recurring series should pass at least two of:

- audience knows what value to expect;
- production becomes meaningfully more efficient;
- the series creates anticipation;
- it develops a narrative over time;
- it enables useful comparison;
- it creates sponsor value without harming editorial quality;
- first-party results justify repetition.

## Series fields

Record:
- series name;
- purpose;
- target audience;
- trigger or cadence;
- format behavior;
- required assets;
- success signals;
- minimum review sample;
- iteration rule;
- retirement rule.

## Avoid

Do not create series such as:
- "Motivational Monday"
- "Throwback Thursday"

unless there is a real Polo Connect audience reason.

Calendar gimmicks are not strategy.

## Visual consistency

Series may have recurring visual cues, but should support content rather than forcing every instance into identical art direction.

For match footage and documentary content, preserve authenticity.
