# Decision framework

## Opportunity Value Score

Use only after eligibility checks.

Score each dimension 0–5.

- `audience_value` — 20%
- `timeliness` — 15%
- `editorial_significance` — 15%
- `polo_connect_advantage` — 15%
- `distinctiveness` — 10%
- `asset_readiness` — 10%
- `strategic_alignment` — 10%
- `benchmark_support` — 5%

Formula:

`opportunity_score = sum(score/5 × weight)`

Interpretation:
- 85–100: top priority
- 70–84: strong
- 55–69: conditional
- 40–54: weak
- below 40: usually reject

A score never overrides hard blockers.

## Decision states

### `produce_now`
Create immediately. The value window is open, evidence is sufficient, assets are ready enough, and the opportunity is stronger than competing candidates.

### `produce_scheduled`
Worth producing for a known future moment.

### `merge`
Combine with another candidate because one stronger piece can serve both audience jobs.

### `story_only`
Useful but too lightweight, repetitive, or transient for a feed asset.

### `monitor`
Potentially valuable, but timing/evidence/context is still developing.

### `hold`
Valid idea, but current queue saturation, strategic timing, or asset quality makes delay preferable.

### `blocked_verification`
Central fact or claim needs the research layer.

### `blocked_assets`
The editorial concept is good, but required media/data/permission is missing.

### `reject`
Low value, expired, duplicative, off-brand, or strategically unnecessary.

## Priority classes

Use:
- `P0` — immediate live/breaking requirement
- `P1` — high-value same-day
- `P2` — scheduled/near-term
- `P3` — evergreen/opportunistic
- `P4` — archive/backlog

Priority is operational, not a quality score.

## Decision discipline

Do not promote candidates because:
- a senior stakeholder mentioned them;
- the subject is famous;
- a benchmark account did something similar;
- there is an unused asset available;
- the calendar looks empty.

Every approved opportunity must satisfy a real audience or strategic job.
