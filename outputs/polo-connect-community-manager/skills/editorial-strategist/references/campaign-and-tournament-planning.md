# Campaign and tournament planning

## Event arc

Major polo events should be treated as narrative systems.

Potential phases:

### Pre-event
- why it matters;
- field/teams;
- player/horse context;
- format;
- history;
- viewing information;
- predictions/questions.

### Event
- arrival/atmosphere;
- live utility;
- decisive moments;
- stats;
- human reactions;
- behind-the-scenes;
- community conversation.

### Post-event
- result;
- consequence;
- best moments;
- quotes;
- analysis;
- champion/finalist story;
- replay;
- records/milestones;
- next-event bridge.

Use only the phases with genuine value.

## Narrative threads

Track recurring threads such as:
- title race;
- team rivalry;
- player form;
- horse performance;
- comeback;
- debut;
- record pursuit;
- family/history;
- changing rankings.

Threads help continuity without forcing daily coverage.

## Campaign architecture

For product/sponsor/brand campaigns define:

- campaign objective;
- audience;
- promise;
- editorial reason to care;
- message hierarchy;
- content phases;
- required assets;
- required CTAs;
- sponsor/product constraints;
- success metrics;
- maximum promotional load.

## Product campaigns

Lead with user value.

Examples:
- watch live;
- see the replay;
- follow statistics;
- discover listings;
- register a team.

Do not lead with internal feature language when the user benefit can lead.

## Sponsor integration

Prefer:
- native integration into valuable coverage;
- ownership of a useful recurring segment;
- tasteful presence;
- clear contractual fulfillment.

Avoid:
- interrupting emotional/high-value content with unnecessary sponsor copy;
- visually overwhelming the editorial identity;
- unmeasured sponsor repetition.

## Tournament debrief

After major events capture:
- what narratives worked;
- which formats worked;
- asset gaps;
- workflow bottlenecks;
- audience questions;
- missed opportunities;
- series worth continuing;
- archive value created.
