# Analysis framework

## Unit of analysis

The primary unit is an individual post, Reel, carousel, Story capture, or other social asset.

Account-level conclusions require repeated evidence across multiple posts.

## Creative dimensions

Evaluate only dimensions relevant to the research question.

### Attention
- opening frame;
- first 1–3 seconds;
- headline;
- curiosity gap;
- immediate payoff;
- human/animal/action focal point;
- pattern interruption.

### Information architecture
- primary fact;
- secondary context;
- hierarchy;
- number of simultaneous messages;
- readability;
- sequencing;
- score/stat treatment;
- use of labels and metadata.

### Visual system
- composition;
- crop;
- negative space;
- graphic density;
- typography behavior;
- color behavior;
- photography/video grading;
- overlays;
- logo/sponsor integration;
- consistency vs variation.

### Motion and edit
- duration;
- cut frequency;
- scene progression;
- replay usage;
- slow motion;
- speed ramps;
- camera angle;
- intro/outro;
- transition logic;
- text timing;
- audio synchronization.

### Narrative
- setup;
- tension;
- payoff;
- reveal order;
- protagonist;
- stakes;
- context;
- emotional beat;
- ending.

### Copy
- opening line;
- sentence length;
- vocabulary;
- tone;
- use of questions;
- information density;
- CTA;
- comments prompt;
- hashtags;
- naming conventions.

### Social-native behavior
- meme structure;
- trend adaptation;
- collaboration;
- creator/athlete participation;
- comments strategy;
- episodic format;
- seriality;
- community language;
- reactive publishing;
- live-event responsiveness.

### Brand integration
- recognizability;
- restraint;
- sponsor handling;
- consistency;
- distinctiveness;
- whether brand presence supports or interrupts content.

### Production
- required assets;
- editing complexity;
- turnaround time;
- dependence on live footage;
- dependence on data;
- repeatability;
- automation potential.

## Reference Utility Score

Use only when prioritization helps. Score each dimension 0–5.

- `evidence_strength` — 20%
- `polo_relevance` — 20%
- `transferability` — 20%
- `polo_connect_fit` — 15%
- `production_feasibility` — 15%
- `novelty_value` — 10%

Formula:

`utility = sum(score/5 × weight)`

Interpretation:
- 80–100: high-priority reference for testing;
- 65–79: useful;
- 50–64: situational;
- below 50: archive unless strategically important.

A score never overrides a material legal, brand, rights, or evidence problem.

If Polo Connect's formal brand system is not available, mark `polo_connect_fit` as provisional and explain the basis.

## Performance signal

Keep separate from Reference Utility Score.

Recommended labels:
- `strong_relative_signal`
- `positive_relative_signal`
- `baseline_or_unclear`
- `weak_relative_signal`
- `insufficient_data`

Prefer within-account and within-format baselines.

## Pattern status

Classify every extracted pattern:

- `single_example` — observed once;
- `repeated_account_pattern` — repeated within one account;
- `cross_account_pattern` — observed independently across accounts;
- `platform_pattern` — supported by broader current platform evidence;
- `polo_connect_validated` — supported by Polo Connect first-party results.

The final state is the most valuable. External popularity should eventually yield to Polo Connect's own evidence.
