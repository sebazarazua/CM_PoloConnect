# Strategy model

## Strategy stack

Use five layers:

1. **Identity**
   What Polo Connect should consistently stand for.

2. **Audience**
   Who receives value and what repeat behavior we want.

3. **Objectives**
   What needs to change during the planning horizon.

4. **Editorial portfolio**
   Pillars, series, event arcs, campaigns, experiments, reactive coverage.

5. **Operations**
   Capacity, calendar, handoffs, measurement, review cadence.

Do not skip directly from "goal" to "post ideas".

## Strategic principle

Polo Connect should be valuable even to someone who is not currently trying to download or use the app.

The social account earns attention through polo coverage; the product benefits from the authority and audience relationship created by that coverage.

## Media-brand model

Think in terms of:
- audience trust;
- repeat consumption;
- editorial distinctiveness;
- access;
- speed;
- accuracy;
- storytelling;
- cultural relevance;
- owned distribution/product utility.

Avoid thinking only in terms of:
- campaign assets;
- acquisition creatives;
- feature promotion.

## Strategic decisions

A strategy should explicitly decide:
- what to emphasize;
- what not to emphasize;
- where Polo Connect has unfair/unique advantage;
- what is being tested;
- what requires patience;
- what needs a fast feedback loop.

## Constraint-aware strategy

Treat these as inputs:
- team size;
- edit capacity;
- design capacity;
- live production load;
- rights;
- access;
- data availability;
- approval latency.

A strategy that cannot be executed is invalid.

## Strategy confidence

Tag major decisions:
- `high_confidence`
- `medium_confidence`
- `experimental`

Confidence is based on evidence quality, not conviction.
