# Editorial Strategist

## Purpose

Maintain a coherent, adaptive editorial system for Polo Connect.

This skill governs the account across days, weeks, tournaments, and campaigns so that individual content opportunities contribute to a recognizable media brand instead of becoming disconnected posts.

It defines:
- strategic objectives;
- audience priorities;
- editorial pillars;
- recurring series;
- campaign and tournament arcs;
- portfolio balance;
- planning windows;
- production capacity allocation;
- platform roles;
- KPI hierarchy;
- feedback rules;
- guardrails for downstream opportunity selection and production.

It does not verify news, research benchmarks in depth, decide one isolated reactive post, design assets, write final captions, edit video, schedule, or publish.

## Core principles

1. Strategy is a decision system, not a fixed posting calendar.
2. Polo Connect should behave like a high-quality polo media brand with product capabilities, not like a software company constantly advertising an app.
3. Human, real-world polo content is the default creative substrate: real matches, players, horses, venues, supporters, production access, interviews, data, and licensed archive.
4. AI may organize, analyze, draft, and accelerate production, but the public result must preserve human taste, factual integrity, and authentic source material.
5. Content pillars are flexible strategic territories, not quotas or boxes that every post must fit.
6. Series are useful only when they create audience recognition or production leverage without becoming repetitive.
7. Platform-wide benchmark averages are priors, not rules. Polo Connect first-party performance should progressively outrank generic studies.
8. Different formats serve different jobs. Never impose a universal Reel/carousel/Story ratio.
9. Editorial consistency means consistent judgment and identity, not identical layouts, hooks, or tones.
10. Planned and reactive content must coexist. A strategy that cannot absorb breaking sport is not a sports-media strategy.
11. Capacity is strategic. The plan must be executable by the actual team and production system.
12. Every strategic choice should be revisable when evidence changes.

## Planning horizons

Maintain distinct horizons.

### Seasonal / tournament horizon
Weeks to months.

Defines:
- strategic themes;
- tournament narratives;
- major campaigns;
- series;
- audience growth priorities;
- production requirements;
- sponsor commitments.

### Monthly horizon
Defines:
- primary objectives;
- pillar emphasis;
- campaign milestones;
- recurring series cadence;
- experiment portfolio;
- capacity allocation.

### Weekly horizon
Defines:
- known matches/events;
- planned stories;
- reactive capacity reserve;
- production order;
- asset capture needs;
- priority experiments.

### Daily / live horizon
Owned primarily by `content-opportunity-detector`.

The strategist supplies constraints and priorities but should not micromanage every reactive post.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish strategic context

Resolve:
- planning window;
- business/media objective;
- audience state;
- event calendar;
- product priorities;
- sponsorship obligations;
- production capacity;
- recent performance;
- known risks.

Distinguish fixed obligations from optional editorial choices.

### 2. Define audience priorities

Identify the audience groups relevant to the period.

Examples:
- existing polo fans;
- casual sports audience;
- people discovering polo;
- tournament attendees;
- players/teams/community;
- horse/equestrian audience;
- viewers seeking live/replay coverage.

Do not assume all content targets the same audience.

For each priority audience, define:
- what they want from Polo Connect;
- what Polo Connect can uniquely provide;
- what would build repeat behavior.

### 3. Define strategic objectives

Choose a small number of primary objectives for the planning window.

Examples:
- grow qualified discovery;
- increase repeat consumption;
- establish authority around a tournament;
- increase live-match viewing;
- increase replay consumption;
- deepen community interaction;
- build recognition of Polo Connect's editorial identity;
- introduce product utility without becoming promotional.

Attach measurable signals using `references/objectives-and-measurement.md`.

Do not declare every metric a primary KPI.

### 4. Configure editorial pillars

Use pillars as flexible territories.

A pillar should answer:
- what audience need it serves;
- why Polo Connect has permission/advantage to cover it;
- what kinds of stories belong;
- what it must not become.

Pillars may be emphasized, reduced, merged, or retired.

Do not assign fixed percentages unless first-party evidence and capacity justify them.

### 5. Define recurring series selectively

Create a series only when repetition adds value through:
- recognition;
- anticipation;
- utility;
- narrative continuity;
- production efficiency;
- sponsorship fit;
- measurable learning.

Every series must have:
- purpose;
- audience;
- trigger/cadence;
- format flexibility;
- continuation criteria;
- retirement criteria.

A series is not a template prison.

### 6. Build event and tournament arcs

For major events, design a narrative arc rather than isolated posts.

Possible stages:
- anticipation;
- context;
- participants;
- access/behind-the-scenes;
- live moments;
- result;
- consequence;
- human reaction;
- analysis;
- archive/legacy;
- replay or next-event bridge.

Use only stages that add audience value.

### 7. Allocate editorial capacity

Set the portion of team/system capacity reserved for:
- planned production;
- reactive/live opportunities;
- evergreen/education;
- experimentation;
- sponsor/product obligations.

Use ranges or production slots rather than rigid post counts when uncertainty is high.

Protect reactive capacity during major tournament windows.

### 8. Set portfolio guardrails

Define temporary guardrails for:
- promotional load;
- repeated subjects;
- tournament dominance;
- feed vs Stories;
- real footage vs graphics;
- planned vs reactive work;
- premium storytelling vs immediate utility;
- experimental vs proven formats.

Guardrails should prevent drift, not force artificial variety.

### 9. Define platform/format strategy

Choose formats by strategic job.

Current platform priors may inform the plan, but do not hardcode them.

Examples:
- Reels for discovery and emotionally strong motion;
- carousels for structured depth/save-worthy information;
- Stories for immediacy, interaction, and lightweight updates;
- single images when one visual/message is genuinely sufficient;
- live/native video when the event and rights justify it.

If Polo Connect data contradicts generic benchmarks, prefer Polo Connect data after checking sample quality.

### 10. Define product integration

Polo Connect's app/platform should appear when it creates user value.

Preferred:
- "Watch this match live on Polo Connect."
- "Full replay available."
- "See live statistics."
- "Save the horse/listing."

Avoid:
- repetitive generic app promotion;
- forcing product CTAs into unrelated editorial content;
- turning every sports post into acquisition advertising.

Editorial trust has long-term product value.

### 11. Define brand behavior

Set period-specific guidance for:
- tone;
- editorial confidence;
- visual range;
- premium vs spontaneous balance;
- humor tolerance;
- language/localization;
- sponsor visibility;
- community behavior.

The account may mutate between elegant, urgent, emotional, data-led, humorous, and documentary modes while remaining recognizable.

### 12. Plan experiments

Maintain a deliberate experiment portfolio.

Each experiment needs:
- hypothesis;
- variable being tested;
- comparable baseline;
- evaluation window;
- success/failure signal;
- decision after measurement.

Do not change five major variables and call the result a test.

### 13. Build the editorial calendar

Calendar known moments, not fictional certainty.

Include:
- fixed events;
- planned series;
- capture requirements;
- campaign milestones;
- sponsor commitments;
- experiment slots;
- open/reactive capacity.

Do not pre-fill every empty day merely to look organized.

### 14. Supply constraints to opportunity detection

Create a strategy brief for `content-opportunity-detector` containing:
- current objectives;
- priority audiences;
- active pillars;
- major narratives;
- saturation warnings;
- campaign obligations;
- reactive capacity;
- experiment opportunities;
- content to avoid/reduce.

This is the main operational handoff.

### 15. Review performance

At defined review points:
- compare outcomes to objective-level KPIs;
- inspect pillar/series performance;
- distinguish format effect from topic/event effect where possible;
- identify audience behavior;
- identify fatigue;
- update hypotheses.

Do not react to one weak post by rewriting the whole strategy.

### 16. Update the strategy

Classify each strategic element:

- `continue`
- `increase`
- `decrease`
- `iterate`
- `pause`
- `retire`
- `test_again`

Document why.

## Hard gates

Do not produce a plan that:
- fixes universal content percentages without evidence;
- fills every day by default;
- prioritizes volume over quality;
- depends on synthetic-looking sports imagery when real/owned material exists;
- treats all audiences as identical;
- treats every tournament moment as a feed post;
- allows sponsors/product messages to overwhelm editorial trust;
- uses benchmark accounts as style templates;
- optimizes for vanity metrics without linking them to an objective;
- ignores actual production capacity.

## Quality bar

A strong strategy should make the account coherent even if individual posts look different.

A team reading the plan should understand:
- who Polo Connect is serving;
- what it is trying to achieve;
- what narratives matter now;
- what deserves repeated investment;
- what should be reduced;
- how much room remains for live sport;
- how success will change future decisions.

If the output is mostly a calendar of post ideas, it is not an editorial strategy.
