# Footage selection

## Shot value dimensions

Rate candidate clips qualitatively on:

- `editorial_value`
- `clarity`
- `emotion`
- `uniqueness`
- `technical_quality`
- `context_value`
- `audio_value`
- `rights_confidence`

Do not reduce selection to technical quality alone.

## Sports-action priorities

For polo action, prefer footage that preserves:
- ball visibility when possible;
- horse/rider relationship;
- speed;
- field direction;
- decisive contact;
- reaction;
- scoreboard/context when useful.

## Camera roles

Potential roles:
- wide/master: comprehension and field geometry;
- tracking/medium: action intensity;
- close: player/horse emotion and detail;
- reverse/replay: clarification;
- crowd/reaction: consequence;
- drone/high angle: scale, venue, tactical context;
- backstage/handheld: intimacy/authenticity.

Do not use every camera simply because it exists.

## Primary vs support clips

Tag:
- `must_use`
- `strong_option`
- `context_only`
- `optional`
- `avoid`

## Technical imperfections

A slightly imperfect owned shot may be worth using when:
- it is exclusive;
- it captures authentic emotion;
- no cleaner shot exists;
- imperfection does not prevent comprehension.

Do not over-clean documentary texture.

## Replays

Use replays when they:
- clarify what happened;
- reveal skill;
- show horse movement;
- create emotional payoff;
- provide an angle unavailable in the live sequence.

Avoid replay repetition that stalls momentum.

## Slow motion

Use for:
- exceptional technique;
- impact;
- emotion;
- horse athleticism;
- decisive contact.

Do not slow ordinary footage merely to feel cinematic.

## Archive

Mark historical footage clearly when necessary.

Do not intercut archive and current match footage in a way that could mislead viewers.
