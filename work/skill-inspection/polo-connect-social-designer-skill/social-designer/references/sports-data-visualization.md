# Sports data visualization

## Principle

Data graphics are editorial claims.

Accuracy and comprehension outrank decoration.

## Source of truth

Every displayed value must map to:
- verified source;
- first-party data;
- approved stat record.

Do not manually infer missing values from visual charts.

## Choose the simplest form

Use:
- big number for one key stat;
- list/table for exact ranking;
- bar for magnitude comparison;
- line for change over time;
- timeline for chronology;
- head-to-head cards for matchup;
- small multiples for comparable players/teams.

Avoid a chart if plain text is clearer.

## Scale integrity

For quantitative charts:
- do not truncate axes deceptively;
- disclose percentages/units;
- keep consistent scales in comparisons;
- label values directly when useful.

## Scoreboards

Scores must prioritize:
- team identity;
- score;
- status/final;
- tournament/stage.

Secondary:
- period/chukker;
- scorers;
- handicap;
- venue.

Do not make sponsor/logo larger than score relevance.

## Rankings

Show:
- ranking body;
- position;
- movement;
- points/value;
- date/currentness if relevant.

Do not combine incompatible ranking systems.

## Schedules

Show:
- teams;
- date;
- local time;
- venue where useful;
- live/viewing availability.

Timezones must be explicit when audience ambiguity exists.

## Statistical storytelling

A stat should answer:
- why is this notable?
- compared with what?
- over what period?
- based on which definition?

"8 goals" alone may be meaningless without context.
