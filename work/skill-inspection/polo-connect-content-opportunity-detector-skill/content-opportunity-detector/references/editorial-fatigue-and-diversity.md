# Editorial fatigue and diversity

## Purpose

Prevent a technically correct queue from becoming repetitive, exhausting, or indistinguishable.

## Fatigue dimensions

Track recent concentration by:
- format;
- topic;
- tournament;
- player/team;
- news type;
- visual structure;
- hook style;
- CTA;
- objective;
- sponsor exposure.

## Repetition is not always bad

Repeat when:
- the event is evolving;
- the audience expects live updates;
- a result follows a preview;
- a recurring series has proven value;
- a new piece serves a different audience job.

Avoid when:
- the new asset adds no new fact, emotion, angle, or utility;
- the same subject dominates because it is easy to produce;
- the same template is being reused without strategic reason.

## Diversity checks

Before approving a queue, ask:
- Are we over-indexing on match results?
- Have we ignored horses/equestrian identity?
- Are we publishing only elite men's polo?
- Are we mixing live, context, people, education, and atmosphere where appropriate?
- Is product promotion crowding out editorial value?
- Are all Reels opening the same way?
- Are all graphics using the same information density?

## Format balance

Do not target a fixed ratio unless Polo Connect first-party data justifies it.

Balance should follow:
- audience jobs;
- event calendar;
- production capacity;
- measured performance.

## Saturation handling

If several strong candidates compete:
1. merge when they serve the same need;
2. move lightweight updates to Stories;
3. schedule evergreen items later;
4. keep the strongest unique feed asset;
5. protect high-value live windows.

## Novelty

Novelty means a meaningfully different audience experience, not merely a different color or crop.
